How the picks are made

The bookmaker's spread, moneyline and total are stripped of their margin and turned into an expected winning margin and an expected total. Those are blended 75/25 (80/20 for the total) with each team's recent points for and against, plus home court, then adjusted for injuries announced after the odds were taken.

The final margin and total are treated as bell curves (wider in the NBA than in Europe), which prices every spread, total, team total and first-half line. Player projections take season averages and scale them to this game's expected pace and the chance the player suits up.

Picks are graded each morning, and once a market has 8 graded picks the model adjusts it, the same way as football.

Reading the numbers

Min odds is the lowest price worth taking: the model's chance, anchored to Pinnacle's price (the sharpest bookmaker), plus 1%. If your bookmaker pays less, skip it. Local bookmakers often pay 5–10% less than Pinnacle.

Value bet appears only when a price beats the min odds after that anchoring, which is rare and usually follows late team news. Most likely is the safest outcome of each game at a fair price: useful for accumulators, but not an edge.

Spreads and totals on whole numbers can push; the chance shown is the chance to win the bet, and the fair price allows for the stake back on a push.

Verdict: Bet (the price beats the model), Lean (about fair, 55%+ chance) or Skip (the price is too short, or the chance is too low). Build slips from Bet and Lean rows only.

Live mode opens on each game once it starts. It pulls the score from a free feed for the NBA and NHL, or takes what you type, and re-prices the game for the time left.

Before you bet

These are probabilities, not certainties. A 60% pick still loses four times in ten, and with odds this close to the market most bets carry no real edge. Never stake more than you can afford to lose. 18+.
